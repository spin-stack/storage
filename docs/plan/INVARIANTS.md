# INVARIANTS

Invariants extracted from the architecture doc (§4/§5/§12/§14/§21/§27). Each is a
property a **checker** verifies inside the DST harness. An invariant is born
`pending` and becomes `active` when its checker runs in CI. The gate (`CLAUDE.md`)
requires all `active` checkers green.

**Checker states:** `pending` (no checker yet) · `active` (checker runs in CI) ·
`withdrawn` (ADR-0026 removed the mechanism the property was about; the row says what
survives and what would bring it back).

> **Rewritten 2026-08-03 against the code.** ADR-0026 removed roughly half the system —
> the remote durability chain, promotion, checkpoints, GC, the lease-gated ACK — and this
> file went on describing checkers, scenarios and packages that no longer exist. Rows
> naming deleted code are worse than missing rows: they are the reason a reader believes
> a property is proven. Every row below was checked against a symbol that exists.
>
> **What an active checker does and does not prove.** Every `active` checker here has
> been shown to catch a *planted bug*, and `TestMain` in `internal/dst` now enforces that
> by recording which proofs actually ran — a registry of claims about proofs is the same
> failure mode as a checker that cannot fire, and two of its six entries were fiction
> until that check existed. Four of the six proofs drive the real `agent.VolumeManager`
> or the real `wal.Log`; the rest are library-level.

## What an invariant's state means here

- **An invariant becomes `active` in the increment that lands its checker**, and that is
  recorded in its row and in `STATUS.md`. Nothing else flips it.
- **`withdrawn` is not `pending`.** Pending means the checker has not been written;
  withdrawn means the *mechanism the property was about* no longer exists, so there is
  nothing to check. Each withdrawn row says what survives of it and what would bring it
  back — deleting the rows would lose exactly that.
- An invariant whose checker cannot yet be expressed stays `pending` and says so in its
  row. Shipping it as prose only is the failure mode: a checker that does not exist and a
  checker that cannot catch a planted bug are the same thing, and 2026-08-03 added a third
  member to that family — *a registry claiming a proof that was never written*.
